Siddhartha and Virtue Signaling

Siddharta is able to cast a spell on a Shaman. The Shaman is cursing Siddharta and his friend. But suddenly in his stillness, Siddharta is somehow able to make the man wish them well and bless them.

Siddharta here is virtue signaling. It may seem initially like they're suggesting SIddharta has some sort of superpower. This isn't the case. The Shaman can see behind Siddharta's eyes that he knows he's correct and virtuous. There isn't a single thing the Shaman could say that would harm Siddharta, and nothing he could do to him.

Siddharta knew he was in the right, and could project it into his being, his posture, and convery to the man that he was wrong to curse him. This gives the appearance of casting a spell, but

This is they key to all conflict.

For example, if you obey your roommate agreement perfectly. Are respectful of their space and communicate often and gracously.

And this is what people mean when they want to manifest something. They mean to act in a genuine manner, showing their true intentions to everyone, so that they can be granted what they wish with no one to feel its undeserved.

This is also a central teaching of the bible. To act virtuously is to live perfectly. Live in perfect balance with nature and yourself.

This 'Agnus' the Konstantin Korobov, Painting. It's to remain pure in an impure world. To show your vulnerability and purity despite the chaos of the world around you. The ideal state of being.

"I saw a man, Siddharta thought, a single man, before whom I would have to lower my glance. I do not want to lower my glance before any other , not before any other. No teachings will entice me anymore, since this man's teachings have not enticed me."

Siddharta is seeking to purify himself to the extent that he feels he will always be more virtuous than anyone else.








This is what Aristotle would call the Golden Mean. Pursuing eudaimonia. Often translated simply as "happiness," eudaimonia more accurately means human flourishing or fulfilling your ultimate potential


Aristotle argued that while we pursue wealth or pleasure as stepping stones, eudaimonia is the highest good, an end in itself, achieved by living excellently over a complete lifetime.


You don't become virtuous by studying philosophy or memorizing laws; you become virtuous through practice. As Aristotle noted, we become just by doing just acts, and brave by doing brave acts. Moral excellence is essentially muscle memory for your character.
